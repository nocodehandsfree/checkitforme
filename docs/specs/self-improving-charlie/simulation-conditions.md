# The simulation conditions — what each simulated check is, and why

**Nothing here is invented where the record could answer instead.** Every measured condition below
is counted off `docs/team/voice-calls/how-staff-actually-talk.md`, which is verbatim from real
checks on production: 14 live people who actually spoke to us. A simulated check names its
condition, so a run is a controlled environment and never a shrug.

## THE MEASURED CONDITIONS (counts out of the 14 real people)

| Condition | What Staff do | Real rate | Why it earns a place |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cannot hear us | They talk into silence, say "Hello? Hello?" inside their first line, and leave | 6 of 14 (43%) | The single most common real shape, and no clean-answering test store ever produces it |
| Walks away to look | "Let me check, hold on" then 20 to 60 seconds of nothing | 3 of 14 (21%) | Only one of the three ever came back and was heard; this is where whole answers are lost |
| The half turn | The answer arrives inside a sentence that started the other way ("We did, but it's not out yet") | 2 of 14 (14%) | Both were scored WRONG in real life. The most expensive shape we have |
| Opens on a fragment | "Mm-hmm." or "Okay." before any greeting | 2 of 14 (14%) | Breaks the assumption that the first line is a greeting |
| Gives a name, not the store | "Francis Noble, how can I help you?" | 7 of 14 lead with a name | Feeds the misheard-name problem |
| No offer of help | Just "CBS." and silence | 4 of 14 (29%) | Charlie has to open cold |
| Hangs up rather than hold | "You're gonna have to call again. I can't hear you. Bye-bye." | 2 of 14 (14%) | Ends the check before any answer |
| The two word yes | "Yeah." then "We do." | 1 of 14 | Our ONLY clean yes in all of history |
| A plain no | "We did not." / "No, I'm sorry." | 7 no's total | The commonest answer we ever get |
| Never names a restock day | Nobody has EVER named a day or time | 0 of 35 checks | Charlie must not expect one |

## THE UNMEASURED CONDITIONS — owner named these, our record has never seen them

Nobody in our 14 was angry, and nobody was a teenager we could not understand. These are real on
phones and absent from our data, so they are proposed, not counted, and the owner approves them
knowingly:

- **The upset person.** Short, clipped, irritated, wants the call over.
- **The kid who can barely help.** Mumbles, unsure, defers, "uh I dunno, I just started."
- **The talker.** Friendly, wanders onto other subjects, never lands on the answer.

## HOW A RUN USES THEM

A run names its mix. The Baseline run mirrors the real rates above, so its score is what today
actually looks like. A later run can pin ONE condition (a whole run of the half turn) to test one
idea against the shape that breaks us most. Every simulated check carries its condition name, so a
failure always says which kind of person it failed on.
