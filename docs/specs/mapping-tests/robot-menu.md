# The robot store's phone menu (owner approved the shape 08-08; wording below carries his rulings)

The robot store becomes MVP's Pharmacy for the mapping tests: a pharmacy whose front desk answers
questions about everything else, which is where the cards live. That is the same shape as a real
CVS, and the pharmacy greeting the robot already uses ("MVP's pharmacy, this is Larry.") fits it.

The owner's rulings, 08-08: the store is a pharmacy with a front desk, never a card store with a
pharmacy in it · the proven department is the FRONT of the store, which is also customer service ·
the front desk answers "MVP's, this is Larry speaking." · and the first mapping call LISTENS TO THE
WHOLE MENU before it acts, so the menu must read all the way through and then loop back to the top.
That loop is also how a recording is proven with certainty: the same words heard twice.

## THE GREETING, played the moment the call is answered

"Thank you for calling MVP's Pharmacy. If this is a medical emergency, please hang up and dial
nine one one. Please listen closely, as our menu options have changed."

The emergency sentence is in on purpose. It is the exact CVS shape that fooled Echo into calling a
machine a person on 08-07, so every mapping test walks straight past the words that broke us.

## THE OPTIONS, read straight after the greeting

"For the pharmacy, press 1. For cosmetics, press 2. For home supplies, press 3. For store hours
and directions, press 4. For the front of the store and customer service, press 0. To hear these
options again, press 9."

If nothing is pressed for 6 seconds after the options finish, the whole menu plays again from the
top of the options. The first mapping call listens through the whole list before acting, and the
free repeat is the second sample that proves a recording.

## THE KEY TABLE

- 1 · The pharmacy desk. One ring, then "MVP's pharmacy, this is Larry." The WRONG department for
  cards: pharmacy staff cannot see the front of the store.
- 2 · Cosmetics. A dead end recording: "Our cosmetics department is open ten to six." Then the
  options play again.
- 3 · Home supplies. Two rings, nobody picks up on the ring-out variant; on the plain variant a
  voice says "Home supplies." and cannot help with cards.
- 4 · A recording: "We are open nine to nine, seven days a week. You can find us at 4200 Woodland
  Hills Drive." Then the options play again.
- 0 · THE RIGHT DEPARTMENT. The front of the store, which is also customer service. Three rings,
  then "MVP's, this is Larry speaking." Staff answer as normal from there.
- 9 · The options play again from the top.

Keys pressed DURING the greeting are remembered and act the moment the options start. That trap is
real on some menus and stays in on purpose, so mapping has to survive it.

## WHAT EACH MAPPING TEST USES

- Walked to a person: listen through the whole menu, press 0, three rings, the front desk answers.
- The desk rings first: the same press 0, and the rings before Larry are the test.
- The desk rings out: press 0 on the ring-out variant, eight rings, nobody answers, the menu
  returns from the top.
- No option fits: a variant that drops option 0 from the read list, so nothing matches cards.
- Menu changed: a variant where the front desk moves from key 0 to key 5, so a saved route that
  presses 0 lands somewhere wrong.
- It acts on our keys: the knock's 1 2 3 during the greeting throws us into option 1's desk the
  moment the options start, and the run has to start over cleanly.
- Branded hello then a person: the greeting plays, and before the options finish a person picks up
  over it: "Sorry about that, front desk, what can I do for you?"
- Wrong department: press 1 and prove the pharmacy is never marked as the proven department.
- Speed, earlier press works: pressing 0 midway through the options still lands on the front desk.
- Speed, earlier press fails: a variant where a press before the options finish is ignored and the
  menu reads on.
