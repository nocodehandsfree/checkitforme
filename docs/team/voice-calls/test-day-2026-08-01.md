# Test day, 08-01 — the owner's first ladder test, for the PM

He ran **test 1 of the six-test ladder five times**. Every run found something real. Seven faults,
nine fixes, all shipped to staging the same day. **Zero ladder tests have passed yet** — run 6 is the
first attempt against the fully fixed engine.

## The five runs, in his words and what each turned out to be

| Run | What he saw on his phone | What it actually was | State |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | He said "I'm gonna put you on hold" and the page instantly said Getting results, then "couldn't tell" — with his phone still connected | Two faults. The phrase test for a wrong department fired on the words "put you" alone, so his hold line read as "we reached the wrong department." And the page asked ElevenLabs whether the check was still running; dropping Charlie for a hold ends his conversation there, which reads as "finished" | Both fixed |
| 2 | The check ended while he stood away from the phone; verdict "Left on hold"; he was charged | The same wrong source, two more doors. Both places that write the final answer (their end-of-call report, and our sweeper) believed the provider's "finished" and stamped a verdict with the phone line still up | Fixed — nothing may write an answer until the phone company says the line ended |
| 3 | Same again after the fix | The customer page swaps to asking about the check by Charlie's session id a few seconds in, and that path had no guard | Fixed — both ways of asking now answer from our own record |
| 4 | His greeting was missing, what was left opened mid-sentence UNDER Charlie's question, and his two sentences merged into one line with two Charlie lines in a row | Three faults. (1) We only started keeping line audio after we were sure a person was there — about half a second in, exactly where a store says its name. (2) His held hello and his next sentence reached Charlie as one unbroken stretch, so they transcribed as one line. (3) The live view printed our question the instant it played, but his words only exist once Charlie transcribes them, seconds later — so the question always showed first | All three fixed — audio kept from the first voiced sound, a real pause inserted between his two turns, question held off the live view until the hello lands |
| 5 | A voice started talking while he wasn't; the question printed twice; the page bounced; then it hung up and said Left on hold | The big one. A fallback timer armed at the start of every call kept ticking after he answered. Sixty seconds in — him on hold, Charlie rightly closed — it fired and opened a SECOND Charlie, blind, into his hold. Separately, Charlie's session echoes our recorded question back as if he just said it, printing it twice | Both fixed — no timer may ever open Charlie once a real person has been found; the echo is dropped |

## Why it took five runs, honestly

The conversation lives in three places — the saved record, the live page, and what Charlie hears —
and the check's "is it still running" is asked through two different names. The first fixes each
covered one door and the next run walked through another. The engine's own log (kept on staging) is
what finally showed each fault exactly; it should be the first stop next time, not the third.
